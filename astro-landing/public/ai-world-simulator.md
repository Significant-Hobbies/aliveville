# AI world simulator in the browser — Aliveville

An AI world simulator is a persistent environment where characters act on their own goals instead of waiting for scripted quest markers.

Aliveville is a browser-playable 3D AI civilization simulation: a town of autonomous agents who remember people, hold grudges, form relationships, and create emergent stories while you explore.

## How it differs from chat NPCs

- Agents keep living when you look away
- Memory and social state across encounters
- 3D town you can walk
- Browser-native public demo

## What a session looks like

- Open the game and drop into the town — agents are already mid-routine
- Talk to a character; they answer with memory of who you are and what happened before
- Watch consequences spread — arguments, favors, rumors change town state
- Come back later: relationships, grudges, and quests persist across visits

## How Aliveville compares

- Aliveville — a 3D town of autonomous agents with memory, playable free in the browser. Small, persistent, open-source.
- World Simulator AI (worldsimulator.ai) — describe a world in text and it generates a playable scenario; generative breadth rather than one persistent town.
- AI Dungeon and text adventures — a narrator that responds to your prompts; the world only moves when you type.
- Research platforms (SimWorld and friends) — built to train and evaluate agents at scale; aimed at researchers, not players.

## Frequently asked

- Is Aliveville free? Yes — the public build is free, no account wall.
- Do I need to install anything? No — modern desktop browser only.
- Do the characters actually remember? Yes — memory and social state persist across encounters.
- Is it still being built? It is an early, opinionated experiment — playable today, bounded in scope.

## Limits

Early opinionated simulator — not a full MMORPG. Agent count and dialogue quality are bounded by the public browser build.
